# Staff Shift Messages — one clear message, no confusion

Most missed shifts are not laziness. The message was unclear: wrong day, no time, no answer needed. A clear message stops the 3 follow-up texts.

## How it works

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  WORKS ALONE                                              │
│  ✓ Tell me the facts. Get a message for the group chat.   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  BETTER WITH                                              │
│  + The week's schedule pasted in                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## What I need from you

The facts: who, which day and time, what job, what changed, by when you need an answer. Tell me the languages your staff read.

## Steps

**Step 1. Pick the message type:**

| Type | Must include |
|---|---|
| Weekly schedule | Name, day, start–end time, role. Post by [day you choose, e.g. Thursday] |
| Cover needed | Day, time, role, pay if extra, "reply by [time]", first yes wins |
| Shift swap | Who, which shift, who approves |
| Late or call-out rule | Whom to call, how early, not text |
| Change of plan | What changed, new time, "reply OK" |
| Thank you | One real thing the person did |

**Step 2. Write the message in the template.** One message, one topic. Max 50 words.

**Step 3. If staff use two languages**, write it twice (short). I will mark where a native speaker should check the translation.

## Output

```
[TYPE]
[First line: what the message is about, in capital letters]
[Day, date, time]
[What you need from them]
[Deadline to reply]

(If bilingual: same message in [second language])

⚠️ BEFORE YOU SEND:
- [Facts I assumed]
- [Translation to check by a native speaker]
```

## Rules (and why)

- **One message, one topic.** Two topics in one message means one gets ignored.
- **Always the full date, not only "tomorrow".** Messages are read late. "Tomorrow" becomes the wrong day.
- **Every request has a reply deadline and a "reply OK" action.** Silence is not a yes.
- **First yes wins for cover.** It stops 5 people answering and 4 feeling bad.
- **Praise in public, correct in private.** A thank-you can go in the group. A warning must be a private chat or call.
- **I never write about pay, hours or firing.** Those are rules and laws. Ask a professional.

## Worked example

Messy input: *"need someone sat dinner 5-close cuz raj sick. extra 15 an hr. need to know by tmrw noon. also reminder sunday we close at 9 not 10"*

Output (two messages, one topic each):
```
COVER NEEDED
SATURDAY DINNER SHIFT, Sat 7 Nov, 5 PM to close
Raj is out sick. Need one person to cover.
Extra $15 per hour for this shift.
Reply "I can" by tomorrow, Fri 6 Nov, 12 PM. First yes gets it.

TIME CHANGE
SUNDAY: WE CLOSE AT 9 PM, not 10 PM
Date: Sun 8 Nov.
Reply "OK" so I know you saw this.

⚠️ BEFORE YOU SEND:
- "Extra $15 an hour": confirm it is above the base rate, not total. Ask your accountant for pay rules.
- Dates assume today is Thu 5 Nov. Check.
```

## Tips

- Pin the weekly schedule in the group chat. People search less.
- Send the schedule on the same day every week. People start to expect it.
- Send one private thank-you message each week. It costs 30 seconds, and it keeps people.

## Quick paste version for ChatGPT

> You write shift messages for my restaurant staff group chat. I will give you the facts. Pick the type: weekly schedule, cover needed, swap, call-out rule, change of plan, or thank you. Write one message for one topic, max 50 words, with the full date and time, what I need from them, and a reply-by deadline. For cover, say "first yes gets it". If my staff read another language, add a short translation and mark it for a native speaker to check. Do not write about pay law or firing. End with a "Before you send" list.
